Journalism is dead, and news went with it. The idea that you can turn on the television, watch the evening news, and walk away with an honest, unbiased account of what's happening in the world was pretty much dead on arrival, and the reason is simple: **they can't afford to tell you the truth anymore.**

News organizations are businesses, and they're in business to make money (shocking, I know). Like every other business they need customers, which for them means viewers, subscribers, advertisers, clicks and eyeballs. Lose those and the lights go out and everybody gets to go update their LinkedIn profiles.

So what do you do when the truth might piss off the people paying your bills? You stop telling all of it. You start choosing which stories get covered, which facts get emphasized, which experts get interviewed, and which inconvenient details get conveniently forgotten, and nobody ever has to lie. All you need is a version of reality that keeps your audience comfortable, angry at the right people and, most importantly, coming back tomorrow, by which point **you're selling validation instead of information.**

Look at CNN, Fox News, ABC, NBC, or MSNBC (which apparently changed its name to MS NOW, or whatever the hell they want to call themselves this week). Every one of them has an audience to hold onto. Fox knows its viewers lean conservative, and CNN and MS NOW know theirs lean liberal. They'll wander outside those boundaries now and then, but there's a powerful financial incentive not to alienate the people who keep showing up.

Imagine Congress announces a $10 billion spending program. On one network, Congress is investing $10 billion to help struggling Americans, and on another it's wasting $10 billion of taxpayer money on yet another bloated government program. It's the same legislation and the same ten billion dollars, told as two completely different stories, so depending on which channel you've got on, you're either celebrating the government's compassion or getting your pitchfork ready to overthrow it.

Fox viewers will swear Fox is telling them the truth, and talk to someone who watches CNN and you'll hear that CNN is the responsible, unbiased alternative. What makes each of them so certain is simply that their favorite news channel agrees with them. **Congratulations, you've confused having your opinions reinforced with being informed.**

Television isn't the only offender, either. Plenty of the same pressures land on online publications, podcasts, YouTube commentators and independent media personalities, although some of them are refreshingly honest about their biases, which I actually respect, because at least they're telling you which direction the bullshit might be coming from.

None of this means every journalist is dishonest. There are still excellent investigative journalists doing difficult, important work, people genuinely trying to dig up corruption and hold powerful institutions accountable. The trouble is a business model that increasingly pays off for whoever gives audiences what they want to hear, with what they need to know coming in second.

Where it gets genuinely dangerous is when millions of Americans are taking in entirely different versions of the same events. At that point we lose the ability to agree on what actually happened, and **the debate stops being about what the facts mean and turns into a fight over which facts even exist.** How the hell are you supposed to run a functioning democracy when half the country thinks the other half is living in an alternate universe?

And we're helping create this mess, even if nobody wants to admit it. The outlets that make us feel right get rewarded and the ones that challenge us get abandoned; the outrageous headlines get our clicks, the inflammatory stories get our shares, and anybody who questions our favorite talking head gets treated like they've committed ideological treason. Then we turn around and complain that journalism has become biased, which, well, no shit, since **we're the ones who trained it to be that way.**

I sometimes wonder whether you'd be better off skipping the news altogether. An uninformed person at least knows there's something they don't know, while being completely misinformed and confidently believing you know everything is arguably worse.

So stop trusting news organizations just because they agree with you. Read the competing accounts too, go find the original documents when you can, figure out which parts are reporting and which are commentary, and ask yourself what a particular outlet might be leaving out.

If your favorite news source keeps telling you that your side is right, the other side is evil, and everything you believed going in was correct all along, then I've got some bad news for you: **you're being sold a product, not informed, and you're apparently a very satisfied customer.**
