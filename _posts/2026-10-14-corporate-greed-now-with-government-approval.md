Apparently, in Louisiana, utility companies have looked at the concept of energy efficiency and discovered a horrifying problem:

**If people use less electricity, the utility companies might make less money.**

Yeah. No shit.

Louisiana has been trying to implement programs designed to help homeowners become more energy efficient ... better insulation, more efficient appliances, improvements that reduce how much electricity a home needs. Pretty normal stuff. The entire point is to help people consume less power and lower their electric bills.

And the utilities have protection against the horrible financial catastrophe of their customers actually succeeding.

Ever since Louisiana's energy efficiency programs started in 2013, the state's big utilities have been allowed to collect something called a "Lost Contribution to Fixed Costs." In 2024 the state tried to replace those programs with a statewide one run by an independent administrator, which would have ended that charge. The utilities argued against it, and in 2025 the Public Service Commission killed it and went back to the old utility-run programs. The rules the commission adopted in January 2026 still say utilities "shall be permitted to recover" it.

Their argument, boiled down to normal English, is essentially this: if these programs cause customers to use less electricity, the utilities lose revenue. So they get a mechanism that recovers some of that lost money through customer rates.

In other words:

**You used less electricity, so now we'd like to charge you because you used less electricity.**

What the fuck kind of business model is this?

Imagine Walmart saying, "Jason, we noticed you only bought $80 worth of groceries this week instead of your usual $120, so here's a $40 lost-revenue adjustment."

Or Exxon sending you a bill because you bought a more fuel-efficient car and aren't buying as much gasoline anymore.

Or you take your car in for an oil change and they slap another twenty bucks on the bill because your car didn't require as much oil as they were hoping to sell you.

You'd laugh in their face.

Because that isn't how selling shit works.

If your customers use less of your product, **you make less money.** That's not some unfair flaw in capitalism. That *is* capitalism. You are not entitled to the money I might have spent with you in some alternate universe where I was more wasteful.

And yet somehow, when you wrap the same ridiculous idea in utility regulations, rate cases, commissions, and enough bureaucratic language to tranquilize a horse, suddenly everybody is supposed to nod along like this makes perfect sense.

It doesn't.

And maybe that's the part that pisses me off the most. Somewhere along the way, expected revenue stopped being something a company hoped to earn and became something it apparently believes it is entitled to receive. If I become more efficient, conserve electricity, and lower the cost of serving my house, that should be a win. Instead, apparently the response is, "Congratulations on saving money. Now give us the money anyway."

You don't get to charge me for electricity I didn't use any more than Exxon gets to invoice me for gasoline I didn't buy.
