*Or, the Mark I Human and the death of intelligent conversation*

Human beings are fascinating creatures. We split the atom, we walked on the moon, and now we've gone and built artificial intelligence, but underneath all that technological sophistication we're still running on the same basic hardware we've had for thousands of years. Call it the Mark I Human Being. It's a tribal, emotional, easily manipulated primate, and more than anything else it wants everything to be simple.

So we boil complicated things down to numbers: four stars versus five, a 72 versus an 85. Five is bigger than four, so five must be better, and congratulations, you've just reduced a complicated evaluation to something a golden retriever could understand. Unfortunately we do exactly the same thing to people. We just use labels instead of numbers.

## The wonderful convenience of othering

There's a concept called *othering*. It's how we sort people into bins (us and them, good and bad, people worth listening to and people we can safely ignore), and nowhere is it more disgustingly obvious than in American politics.

Right now one of the anti-Trump crowd's favorite tricks is calling Donald Trump a pedophile. They don't mean a man whose documented relationship with Jeffrey Epstein deserves scrutiny, or someone facing allegations worth examining. They mean pedophile, case closed.

Never mind that an accusation isn't a conviction, or that the publicly available evidence hasn't established that Trump committed that crime. If evidence proves otherwise, prosecute the bastard. I don't care what political party he belongs to.

But ask someone making that accusation for proof and you get "It's in the Epstein files!" Really? Which file, which page? Have you actually read it, or did some dipshit on TikTok tell you it was there? And if the evidence hasn't been released, well, apparently that just proves it's being hidden. How fucking convenient. We've invented a system where the absence of evidence is itself evidence, which isn't reasoning so much as religion with worse music.

## The label is the argument

What really pisses me off is that the label was never meant to describe a conclusion; it's there to stop you from questioning the conclusion. Disagree about immigration and you're a racist. Question a transgender sports policy and you're a transphobe, push back on a feminist argument and you're sexist, and if you ask for proof of a criminal accusation you're a pedophile defender. And look at that, everybody has successfully avoided discussing the actual subject.

Obviously racists, sexists, bigots, and criminals exist. That's not the fucking point. Words have meanings, and when you throw them around like confetti at a political convention you may think you're demonstrating your moral superiority, but what you're really demonstrating is intellectual laziness. If someone is racist, explain why. If someone committed a crime, show the evidence. Otherwise you're just throwing a tantrum with a vocabulary.

## You can't reason someone out of a position they never reasoned themselves into

I've written before about [why you should never wrestle with a pig](/post/its-okay-to-be-ignorant-just-dont-be-stupid/): you both get dirty, and the pig enjoys it. More and more, that's what political discussion feels like. You present evidence and get outrage, ask questions and get accusations, point out a contradiction and suddenly you're the problem. They don't want a conversation. They want compliance, which in practice means hearing their own opinion come out of your mouth, and if you refuse to parrot whatever bullshit their political tribe has declared to be gospel then congratulations, you're now one of *them*.

I'm seeing this constantly from the loudest voices on the Democratic left. The same people preaching tolerance, diversity, and inclusion are awfully intolerant of diversity of thought; diversity is wonderful, apparently, right up until somebody disagrees with you, and then you need a label so you can get rid of the bastard.

Republicans do it too, of course. They've got their own collection of labels (communist, groomer, traitor, anti-American), and it's the same bullshit on a different bumper sticker. But I'm not going to pretend every criticism has to be handed out in equal portions just to keep everybody happy.

## The death of conversation

The real danger is that we're building a country where people don't disagree with one another anymore. They just declare each other evil. Republicans stop talking to Democrats because they're tired of being called Nazis, Democrats stop talking to Republicans because they've convinced themselves anyone in a red hat is Hitler with a mortgage, and nobody learns a goddamn thing.

A functioning society doesn't need everybody to agree; it needs people who can disagree without instantly deciding the person across the table is a monster. So try asking a question once in a while. Consider that your political party gets things wrong now and then, and entertain the terrifying possibility that somebody you disagree with actually knows something you don't. Radical shit, I know.

We're still Mark I humans, running tribal software on prehistoric hardware. That doesn't mean we have to hand every conversation over to our worst instincts. Bring me facts and evidence and some actual reasoning, or hell, just bring an opinion, because I'm perfectly willing to entertain one. But if all you've got is one more recycled insult or an accusation you can't back up, plus the usual demand that everyone agree with you, then you can fuck right off. A label isn't an argument and outrage isn't evidence, and being absolutely certain you're right doesn't make you anything more than confidently full of shit.
