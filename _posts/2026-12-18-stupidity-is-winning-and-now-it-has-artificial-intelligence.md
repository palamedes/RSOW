Stupidity is winning.

And no, I don’t mean people are suddenly getting dumber. I mean something considerably more dangerous. **People are becoming increasingly willing to let someone, or something, do their thinking for them.**

More than 80 years ago, a German pastor named Dietrich Bonhoeffer watched one of the most educated nations on Earth march enthusiastically toward its own destruction.

Bonhoeffer openly opposed Hitler, became involved in the German resistance, and was eventually executed by the Nazis in April 1945, just weeks before Germany surrendered.

But in 1942, he wrote something that I think might be one of the most important observations about human behavior ever put on paper.

**“Stupidity is a more dangerous enemy of the good than malice.”**

Not evil. Not hatred. Not greed.

Stupidity.

And here’s where it gets interesting. Bonhoeffer wasn’t talking about people who couldn’t pass a math test. He was talking about intelligent, educated people who had surrendered their ability to think independently.

He watched doctors, professors, clergy, and ordinary citizens embrace the Nazi regime. These weren’t [ignorant people](/post/its-okay-to-be-ignorant-just-dont-be-stupid/). Germany was a nation of extraordinary scientific and cultural achievement.

So how the hell did so many people go along with something so monstrous?

Because belonging to the group had become more important than thinking for themselves.

And the terrifying part?

They didn’t think they’d stopped thinking. They thought they were more enlightened than ever.

Sound familiar?

Look at politics today. Pick a party. I genuinely don’t give a shit which one. People routinely defend behavior from their own side that they’d absolutely condemn if the other side did it.

Education doesn’t necessarily fix that. **You can have three doctoral degrees and still be an intellectual coward.**

## And Then We Invented Artificial Intelligence

There’s a wonderful line in the original 1982 movie Tron. Dr. Walter Gibbs sarcastically remarks:

“Won’t that be grand? Computers and the programs will start thinking and the people will stop.”

Holy shit, was that prophetic.

We now have artificial intelligence capable of answering questions, explaining complicated subjects, researching history, writing software, and teaching practically anything.

I’m a software engineer. [I use AI constantly](/post/if-you-cant-beat-the-robots-build-a-nail-gun/). I think it’s an extraordinary technology.

But I also think we’re creating the greatest intellectual crutch in human history.

Don’t understand something? Ask AI.

Need to argue with somebody? Have AI write your response.

Want to [sound knowledgeable about something you’ve never studied](/post/welcome-to-mount-stupid-the-view-is-fantastic-and-nobody-knows-shit/)? Ask AI to produce five beautifully written paragraphs.

Don’t bother checking the sources or questioning the assumptions. Just copy, paste, and congratulate yourself on being informed.

And if the AI gets something wrong?

Well, congratulations. **Now you’re confidently wrong with excellent grammar.**

A 2025 Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon study found that greater confidence in AI was associated with less reported critical-thinking effort among knowledge workers.

That’s not proof AI makes people stupid. But it ought to concern us.

**Because the problem isn’t artificial intelligence. It’s artificial intelligence replacing human intelligence instead of supplementing it.**

You can use AI to challenge your assumptions, explore opposing arguments, and become a considerably better thinker.

Or you can use it to avoid thinking altogether.

Same technology. Two completely different outcomes.

And unfortunately, the second option is so much easier.

## We’ve Industrialized Intellectual Laziness

Bonhoeffer described people surrendering their judgment to political and social authority.

Today, we’re surrendering it to machines that produce convincing answers in seconds.

And unlike political leaders, these machines are available 24 hours a day, sitting in our pockets, ready to tell us something that sounds perfectly reasonable.

Sometimes they’re right. Sometimes they’re wrong. Sometimes they’re completely full of shit.

But they almost always sound convincing.

We’ve reached a point where the appearance of intelligence is becoming cheaper than the real thing.

Bonhoeffer understood that stupidity wasn’t necessarily cured by more information. Overcoming it required something deeper: the willingness to think independently.

And that requires courage.

The courage to question your own side. To admit you’re wrong. To entertain an argument you don’t like.

And increasingly, the courage to tell an AI, “That sounds reasonable, but prove it.”

So here’s my question.

When was the last time you genuinely changed your mind about something important? When was the last time you challenged something your own political tribe believed?

And when was the last time you used AI to challenge your beliefs instead of simply reinforcing them?

Bonhoeffer warned us about surrendering our judgment more than 80 years ago. Tron warned us about machines doing our thinking more than 40 years ago.

And here we are, with the most powerful information technology humanity has ever created, apparently determined to use it to avoid the inconvenience of thinking.

The real danger isn’t that artificial intelligence will become smarter than us.

**It’s that we’ll become perfectly comfortable letting it think for us.**

End of line.
