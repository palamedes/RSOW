Every election, this meme comes back around:

**"Four people want pizza. One person wants an old shoe. Because she's from Wyoming, everyone eats the shoe."**

It's clever.

It's also built on a complete misunderstanding of what the Electoral College actually is.

The meme assumes America is one giant democracy where 340 million people pile their votes into a single national bucket and whoever grabs the most wins.

That's not the country we built.

## We Are a Union of States. It's Right There in the Name.

The United States is exactly what the name says it is: a union of states.

California isn't a county. Texas isn't a county. Wyoming isn't a county. They're sovereign states that agreed to form a federal republic. The President doesn't get elected by one giant national headcount. He gets elected by the states, through a system that balances raw population against state representation.

That wasn't an accident. It was the compromise that made the Constitution possible in the first place.

Without it, the biggest states could permanently steamroll the smallest ones — and the smallest ones would've had no reason to join the Union at all. Why sign up for a club where you're outvoted forever on day one?

## A Better Analogy Than Pizza

The pizza thing falls apart because it imagines five people picking a restaurant.

Try five companies forming a partnership instead.

One company is huge. The other four are much smaller.

Should the biggest one get to pick the CEO forever, just because it has the most employees?

Of course not. The smaller companies would never sign that deal. So they negotiate something else — the big partner gets more influence, but not total control. Everybody has a seat. Nobody gets absolute rule.

That's the Electoral College.

## And No, Wyoming Doesn't Outweigh California

This is the part the meme quietly skips.

California currently has 54 electoral votes. Wyoming has 3.

California has **eighteen times** the influence.

Wyoming doesn't outweigh anybody. The Electoral College just keeps Wyoming from getting rounded down to zero.

## Now Actually Abolish It. Then What?

Say we scrap it tomorrow. Where do presidential candidates campaign?

Not Wyoming. Not Montana. Not the Dakotas. Not Idaho. Not Mississippi. Probably not even most of North Carolina.

They'd pour every dollar into Los Angeles, New York, Chicago, Houston, Dallas, Phoenix, Atlanta, Miami, and Philadelphia. Run up the score in the biggest metros and go home. That becomes the entire strategy, because that's where the votes are stacked.

Rural America wouldn't lose its vote.

It would lose its relevance.

The Electoral College forces candidates to build coalitions that are geographically broad instead of just densely packed. That's the whole reason Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Michigan, Georgia, Arizona, Nevada, and North Carolina matter every four years. Candidates have to talk to people all over the country — not just where the population piles up highest.

## The Part That's Actually Ironic

The people who hate the Electoral College usually say they want everyone's voice to matter.

The Electoral College exists precisely because it makes every state matter.

You can absolutely argue there are better ways to elect a president. That's a real debate, and it's worth having.

But if your argument is a meme about eating an old shoe, you're not criticizing the Electoral College.

You're criticizing a system that has never existed.
