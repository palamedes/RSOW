We've been told for years that toxic masculinity is the problem. That boys need to be less aggressive, less competitive, less assertive, less masculine.

Bullshit.

Maybe the problem isn't that we have too much masculinity. **Maybe the problem is that we've gotten so goddamn uncomfortable with masculinity that we've forgotten how to teach boys to become good men.**

Now, before somebody starts screaming about misogyny, let's get something straight. Nobody is defending abusive men, bullies, predators, or assholes. Those people exist, and their behavior absolutely deserves condemnation.

But since when did being an asshole become an exclusively masculine characteristic?

Strength isn't toxic. Courage isn't toxic. Competition isn't toxic. Protectiveness isn't toxic. Neither is taking responsibility, standing up for yourself, or being willing to do difficult things.

And yet somewhere along the way, we've managed to package an entire collection of masculine characteristics under a label that makes being male sound like a condition requiring treatment.

**Here's what bothers me.**

Millions of boys are growing up without a father living in their home. Schools are overwhelmingly staffed by women, particularly in elementary education. And before anybody gets offended, this isn't an attack on single mothers or female teachers. Many of them are doing extraordinary jobs, often under difficult circumstances.

But is it really controversial to suggest that boys also benefit from having good men in their lives?

Men who demonstrate that strength comes with responsibility. That courage doesn't mean recklessness. That being tough doesn't mean being emotionally dead inside. That you can be competitive without being cruel, confident without being arrogant, and protective without being controlling.

If there are no men in their lives, then who's teaching the boys those things?

And what happens when the most visible examples of masculinity they encounter are some jackass influencer renting a Lamborghini and explaining that women are property?

**That's not masculinity. That's insecurity with a marketing department.**

Meanwhile, we have legitimate problems affecting men and boys. They struggle in education. Men are disproportionately represented in suicide deaths. Many boys grow up without consistent male role models.

And somehow our cultural conversation keeps circling back to what men are doing wrong.

**Maybe we should spend a little more time asking what we're failing to do for them.**

Yes, there are unhealthy expectations associated with traditional masculinity. [Telling boys never to cry, never to ask for help, and never to admit fear](/post/be-a-man-just-dont-act-human/) can be genuinely destructive.

But the answer isn't to eliminate masculinity, like we are actively trying to do, but rather maybe **it's to teach a better version of it.**

We don't teach children that intelligence is toxic because some intelligent people are manipulative. We don't condemn ambition because some ambitious people are ruthless. We teach people to use those qualities responsibly.

Why the hell should masculinity be any different?

A good man isn't someone who has abandoned every traditionally masculine quality. He's someone who has learned when and how to use those qualities.

We should be raising boys who are capable of being dangerous when absolutely necessary, but disciplined enough not to be dangerous to the people around them. Boys who know how to fight but would rather resolve a disagreement. Boys who can shoulder responsibility without believing they must carry every burden alone.

And none of that requires diminishing women, dismissing femininity, or pretending men are superior.

It simply requires recognizing that **there's nothing inherently wrong with being male and with being masculine.**

Maybe instead of treating masculinity like a disease we need to eradicate, we should remember that it can be something worth cultivating.

Because boys don't need another lecture about everything that's wrong with being a man.

**They need good men to show them everything that's right about becoming one.**
