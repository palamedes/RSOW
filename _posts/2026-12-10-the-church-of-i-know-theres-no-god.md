I have a really good friend who's deeply religious. Several, actually, and every one of them has something I've never had, which is faith.

I don't mean the wishy-washy "Well, I think there's probably a God" kind of faith, either. I mean absolute, unquestionable certainty. Ask them whether God exists and they won't hesitate, because they don't just *believe* God exists. They **know** He does.

Here's something I don't often admit: I'm a little jealous. Seriously, imagine having that kind of certainty about something as enormous as existence itself, about why we're here and what happens when we die and whether there's anything beyond this brief, occasionally wonderful, frequently ridiculous experience we call life. It must be comforting, like wrapping yourself in a warm blanket on a cold night. I don't have that blanket and never have. Maybe I'm too analytical or too skeptical, or maybe I'm just a cynical bastard who's spent too much time asking questions and not enough time accepting answers, but whatever the reason, I simply don't possess that kind of faith. **I wish I did.**

## Atheists say faith makes you stupid

This is where things get interesting. More than a few times I've talked with atheists who hear somebody express absolute faith in God and conclude, right away, that the person must be stupid.

Really? I've worked with these religious people and spent a silly amount of time around them, and I've had deep, fascinating conversations with them. Some of them are exceptionally intelligent, accomplished people, and stupid is not a word I'd use to describe any of them. They simply believe something I don't know to be true. The really interesting part is that not one of my deeply religious friends has ever tried to force their religion on me, not once. I've never been dragged to church or cornered with a Bible, and nobody's threatened me with eternal damnation because I didn't accept Jesus before dessert. They believe what they believe, I ask questions, we have interesting conversations, and somehow civilization survives. Imagine that.

I wish I could say the same about some of the atheists I've encountered, because holy shit, some of these people wield atheism like a club. Disagree with them and you're stupid; believe in God and you're ignorant. Admit you're not sure and, well, apparently you just haven't been enlightened enough to understand their superior intellectual position. **Congratulations, you've replaced the church pulpit with a Reddit account.**

## Welcome back to Mount Stupid

Remember [our discussion about the Dunning-Kruger effect and Mount Stupid](/post/welcome-to-mount-stupid-the-view-is-fantastic-and-nobody-knows-shit/), the idea that people with limited knowledge sometimes can't recognize the limits of their own understanding? It comes to mind every time somebody announces they've definitively solved the greatest mystery in human existence, whether the answer they've landed on is that God absolutely exists or that He absolutely doesn't. You've got the whole thing figured out? I'm sitting over here with about ten thousand questions, and apparently both sides have people who already know all the answers.

Now, to be fair, atheism isn't automatically a religion. Saying "I don't believe there's a God" is a position about belief, and saying "I know there is no God" is a much bigger claim about reality, and that's a legitimate distinction.

But when somebody treats the second claim as unquestionable truth, demands intellectual conformity, and mocks the heretics who disagree, what exactly makes their behavior different from the religious fundamentalists they love criticizing? **The doctrine's different and the arrogance is the same.** Mount Stupid doesn't check your religious affiliation before letting you climb aboard.

## So what the hell am I?

Agnostic is probably the closest label. I don't know whether there's a God, and I'm as open to the possibility that there is one as I am to the possibility that there isn't. I've got questions about religion, about science, about existence, plus a few I haven't figured out how to ask yet, and I'm perfectly comfortable admitting that.

I also think religion gives humanity something genuinely valuable: community, purpose, moral frameworks, comfort in suffering, and hope when life becomes unbearable. Yes, it's been used to justify some absolutely horrific things, and I'm not pretending otherwise, but humans have managed atrocities in the name of secular ideologies too. **Turns out human beings are remarkably capable of being assholes regardless of what they believe about the afterlife.** Maybe people need something larger than themselves to believe in, and maybe that's God, or maybe it's humanity, science, family, or some greater purpose. I don't have the answer.

What I do know is that I've met religious people who are humble, thoughtful, generous, and extraordinarily intelligent, and atheists who are exactly the same. I've met insufferable jackasses in both camps, too.

Which brings me to the real point. **I don't think faith makes somebody stupid, and I don't think the absence of faith makes somebody intelligent.** Intelligence should involve recognizing what you know and what you don't, and being willing to entertain the possibility that you're wrong. If your whole worldview depends on dismissing everybody who disagrees with you as an idiot, perhaps you haven't actually climbed down from Mount Stupid at all, and you've just planted your own flag at the summit. And if you need to beat people over the head with your absolute certainty that there's no God, then congratulations: **you may have given up religion, but you sure as hell haven't given up preaching.**
