Somewhere along the way, we decided that the horn on a vehicle was the automotive equivalent of telling somebody to go fuck themselves.

It isn't. Or at least, it shouldn't be.

Today, while riding my motorcycle, I noticed a truck preparing to pull out into traffic. The driver was looking to his right, watching traffic coming from that direction, while slowly inching forward. The problem, of course, was that I was coming from the other direction.

So I honked my horn.

Not because I was angry. Not because I thought he was an idiot. Not because I wanted to express my dissatisfaction with his driving abilities. I simply wanted him to turn his head and acknowledge my existence because, quite frankly, I had no desire to become a hood ornament on his truck.

He stomped on his brakes, and as I passed, I watched in my rearview mirror as he threw his hands up and gave me the one-finger salute.

The funny thing is, I don't entirely blame him.

**We've conditioned people throughout their entire driving careers to interpret the sound of a horn as an act of aggression.** Someone honks, and our immediate reaction is to get defensive. We assume they're insulting us, criticizing us, or questioning our intelligence.

But that's not what a horn is for. It's an audible warning device. Its fundamental purpose is to communicate with people who may not otherwise be aware of your presence.

**"Hey, I'm here."** That's it. That's the entire conversation I was attempting to have with this gentleman.

I don't have the luxury of rolling down my window and politely explaining that I'm approaching from his left. I can't send him a fucking text message. I have a little button on my handlebars that makes noise, and that's precisely why it's there.

Now, obviously, there are people who abuse their horns. We've all encountered the asshole who lays on his horn because the light turned green a quarter of a second ago. There are absolutely people who use their horns as an expression of anger and frustration. But that doesn't mean every use of a horn is an act of hostility.

What's particularly ridiculous is that we've created a culture in which people sometimes hesitate to use a legitimate safety device because they're worried about offending somebody. Think about how absurd that is. **We're potentially compromising our own safety because someone might interpret an audible warning as a personal insult.**

On a motorcycle, that distinction becomes especially important. I'm considerably smaller than everything else on the road, considerably harder to see, and considerably more likely to come out on the losing end of a misunderstanding.

**I'd rather hurt your feelings than have you break my legs.**

So the next time somebody honks their horn at you, before you get pissed off, throw your hands in the air, or start waving your middle finger around, consider the possibility that they're simply trying to communicate something to you. Maybe there's something you haven't seen. Maybe there's a danger you're unaware of. Maybe they're trying to prevent you from making a mistake that neither of you wants to deal with.

**Not everything is a personal attack. Not every interaction is a confrontation.**

Sometimes a honk doesn't mean "fuck you."

**Sometimes it just means "please don't fucking kill me."**
