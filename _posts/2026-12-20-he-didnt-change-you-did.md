I'm a conservative. I don't hate liberals. I don't hate Democrats. I don't hate people on the left.

I think they're wrong about a lot of things. I think some of them are horribly misinformed. And I suspect plenty of them think exactly the same thing about me.

Fine. That's how disagreements work. We're supposed to have different opinions. That's kind of the whole point of living in a free society.

But increasingly, I'm getting the impression that for some people on the left, simply disagreeing isn't enough anymore.

They actually hate us.

And I recently watched a video that illustrated this better than just about anything I've seen.

## Meet Sarah.

That's not her real name, but it doesn't matter.

Sarah was talking about a coworker. Somebody she'd known, worked with, and respected. She described him as extremely intelligent, a brilliant guy working in climate technology.

Then she discovered something horrifying — he was a conservative.

Oh, the humanity.

She explained how surprised and disgusted she was. But what really caught my attention was her admission that she suddenly felt hatred toward this man. Somebody she'd respected just moments earlier.

She actually said, "I hate him now."

Wait a fucking minute.

What changed?

Did he suddenly become stupid? Did his work become less valuable? Did his intelligence evaporate? Did he start kicking puppies in the parking lot?

No.

**He didn't change at all. She did.**

This man was exactly the same person he'd been five minutes earlier. Same intelligence. Same character. Same work ethic. Same accomplishments.

The only thing that changed was that Sarah discovered he belonged to a [political category she despised](/post/your-label-isnt-an-argument-asshole/).

And just like that, years of respect went straight into the garbage.

Here's a question for Sarah: If this man's political beliefs are so obviously incompatible with intelligence and decency, how did you manage to respect him for so long?

Hell, he was apparently intelligent enough to fool your entire political stereotype simply by being himself.

Doesn't that tell you something?

## When Disagreement Becomes Hatred

This is what bothers me about the political climate we're living in.

It's no longer enough to believe somebody is wrong. Increasingly, people seem to believe that being wrong makes somebody morally defective. That disagreement isn't merely evidence of a different perspective, but evidence of a bad person.

And once you've convinced yourself that somebody is a bad person, treating them badly becomes remarkably easy.

We saw an especially ugly version of this after [Charlie Kirk was murdered in September 2025](/post/they-shot-the-messenger-now-we-are-the-message/).

There were people online making jokes, rationalizing his assassination, and even celebrating it.

I'm sure there are plenty of people on the left that condemn his murder, and they deserve credit for doing so. I'm not suggesting every liberal celebrated it.

But the ones who did? Think about what that means.

We're no longer talking about disagreeing over taxes, immigration, abortion, or healthcare. We're talking about people finding satisfaction in another human being being murdered because they didn't like his political opinions.

Charlie Kirk was a husband. A father. A human being.

**You didn't have to agree with a single thing he ever said to recognize that his murder was horrifying.**

And if you can't recognize that, I don't think politics is your biggest problem.

## The Political Closet

Dennis Prager closed the video with an observation that really resonated with me.

He talked about what happens when conservatives stop hiding their political beliefs. You may lose friends, he explained, but you may also discover people who share your values. He calls them "kindred spirits."

And he argues that the tradeoff can be worth it, and I think he's onto something.

But I also think there's something profoundly screwed up about the fact that we're even having this conversation.

Why should anybody have to *come out of the closet* as a conservative?

We're not talking about confessing to a crime. We're talking about political beliefs held by millions of ordinary Americans.

People you work with. People who fix your cars, teach your children, build your houses, and develop the technology you use every day.

Some of them are probably among the smartest, kindest, most generous people you know.

But apparently, for people like Sarah, all it takes to erase that is discovering how they vote.

For what it's worth, conservatives aren't immune to this nonsense either. I've seen plenty of people on the right write somebody off simply because they're liberal.

**That's bullshit, too. Don't do that.**

But here's the difference between disagreeing with someone and hating them: **Disagreement leaves room for the possibility that you might be wrong. Hatred doesn't.**

And that's a dangerous place for a society to end up.

## What Does That Say About You?

I don't need my friends to agree with me politically. I don't even need them to understand why I believe what I believe.

I'd certainly prefer they be willing to discuss it.

But if somebody discovers I'm conservative and suddenly decides I'm no longer worthy of their friendship, respect, or basic human decency, then what exactly did we have in the first place?

And maybe Dennis Prager is right. Maybe losing that friendship isn't such a terrible loss.

Because here's what I keep coming back to.

Sarah didn't discover that her coworker was an idiot. She didn't uncover some horrible secret about his character. She didn't learn that he'd mistreated somebody or committed some terrible act.

She discovered he was a conservative.

And in that moment, she told us considerably more about herself than she ever told us about him.

**He didn't become a worse person when she discovered his politics.**

**She just revealed how little her respect for him was actually worth.**
