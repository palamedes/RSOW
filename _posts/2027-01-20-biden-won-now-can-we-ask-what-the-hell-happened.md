Let's put this fucking question to rest once and for all.

Joe Biden won the 2020 presidential election.

There. I said it. He was certified, inaugurated, and spent four years sitting in the Oval Office napping on the job. (I've written about how [the people around him did him an incredible disservice](/post/they-failed-joe-biden-and-they-failed-america/), but that's an aside.) But whatever, Georgia even counted its presidential ballots three separate times, including a statewide hand tally, and confirmed his victory.

Apparently, that's supposed to be the end of the conversation.

Except I have questions.

And here's a crazy thought: **Aren't we SUPPOSED to question our government?**

Not just when the other team is running it. Not just when our preferred candidate loses. But rather, all the damn time?

Because six years later, we're still discovering and debating irregularities from the 2020 election, and some of them are pretty serious.

In Fulton County, Georgia, officials acknowledged that tabulator tapes covering approximately 315,000 early votes were missing required signatures. Investigators identified thousands of duplicate ballot images from a recount, although whether those images represented additional counted votes remains disputed. There have also been problems with missing digital ballot records.

Does any of that prove Trump actually won? No. But there's clearly some shenanigans.

**And since when did proving an entire election was stolen become the minimum requirement for investigating whether somebody screwed it up?**

Imagine your bank telling you that your account balance is correct, but they lost some transaction records, accidentally processed certain transactions twice, and forgot to sign off on their audits.

Would you just shrug and say, "Well, the manager certified it, so I guess we're good"?

Hell no.

You'd want somebody to open the books and figure out what the hell happened.

And we're talking about something considerably more important than your checking account.

Now, here's where the political hypocrisy becomes particularly entertaining.

Remember 2016?

Hillary Clinton lost to Donald Trump. She conceded the election, and that's an important distinction. But in 2019, she was calling Trump an "illegitimate president" and suggesting the election wasn't on the level.

We spent years discussing Russian interference, election legitimacy, and whether Trump really deserved to be president.

And you know what? **Those questions were fair game!**

Foreign interference deserved investigation. Claims about election integrity deserved scrutiny. The evidence mattered then, and it matters now.

But somehow, when Biden won in 2020, asking uncomfortable questions became a character defect. Suddenly, people were expected to accept the official conclusion and shut the hell up.

That's horseshit.

And Republicans don't get a free pass either. If you're demanding investigations when your guy loses but dismissing legitimate concerns when he wins, you're playing exactly the same stupid game.

**Election integrity isn't supposed to have a party affiliation.**

Look, 2020 is over. Nobody is climbing into a DeLorean and going back to November to fix it. Biden served his term. The Electoral College result wasn't changed. For good or bad, it's over.

But that's precisely why we should be able to investigate every documented screwup without everybody having a political aneurysm.

Require proper chain-of-custody documentation. Reconcile ballot counts. Preserve election records. Conduct independent audits. Make the process transparent enough that ordinary Americans can understand what happened without needing a forensic accountant, three lawyers, and a congressional subpoena.

And when officials fail to follow the law, hold them accountable.

That doesn't mean every irregularity is fraud. It doesn't mean every mistake changes an election. And it certainly doesn't mean every losing candidate gets to declare himself the winner.

It means our elections should be held to a higher standard than "Trust us, bro."

I don't need somebody to convince me Biden was certified. I already know that.

I want a system where the evidence is accessible, the procedures are followed, the mistakes are corrected, and legitimate questions get legitimate answers.

Because **certification tells us who gets the keys to the White House. It shouldn't mean we're required to throw away the receipts.**

Otherwise, we risk arriving at a place described by an old saying, often attributed to Joseph Stalin:

**"It's not who votes that matters, but who counts the votes."**

Whether Stalin actually said it is debatable. The warning behind it isn't.

**And I don't ever want America to become a country where that stops being a warning and starts being a description.**
