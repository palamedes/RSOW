Someone posed a question on Reddit recently:

*“Would you be willing to pay seven dollars out of every one of your paychecks so that children throughout the entire country would never go hungry during their school lunch? No matter the salary of the parents.”*

My answer was no.

Well, apparently, I'm a horrible human being. I'm selfish, uncaring, and presumably spend my evenings sitting in a dark room plotting ways to deprive children of sandwiches.

**Except, you asked me a question. I answered it.**

What became immediately apparent was that this was never a good-faith question. It was an invitation to participate in a collective exercise in virtue signaling. There was only one acceptable answer, and anyone who deviated from that answer was to be publicly castigated.

So why the hell did you bother asking?

I'm not a particularly big fan of public education, or what I've increasingly come to regard as the [indoctrination factory](/post/history-is-not-a-grievance-factory/). I'm a proponent of school choice. I support homeschooling. And I fundamentally believe that parents should be responsible for raising, educating, clothing, and feeding their children.

I don't have kids for a reason. Having children is an enormous financial, emotional, and personal responsibility. I made a conscious decision not to take on that responsibility. **Why does your decision to take it on automatically become my financial obligation?**

And before somebody inevitably asks, no, I don't want children to go hungry. I also recognize that some parents fall on hard times through absolutely no fault of their own. There is a perfectly reasonable discussion to be had about helping those families and protecting children from circumstances they cannot control.

But that wasn't the question, was it?

The question specifically stipulated that the income of the parents was irrelevant. Apparently, I should be financially responsible for feeding the children of a couple making $400,000 a year, too.

**At what point are we addressing poverty, and at what point are we simply subsidizing parenthood?**

This is one of my biggest problems with authoritarian thinking, regardless of which political ideology it comes packaged in. Somebody identifies something they believe to be a moral good, attaches a dollar sign to it, and then declares that anyone who objects to being compelled to fund it is morally deficient.

And it's always just a little bit of money, isn't it?

It's only seven dollars. It's only another dollar in taxes. It's only a small increase. Individually, every proposal sounds perfectly reasonable when presented in isolation. Nobody seems particularly interested in discussing the cumulative burden of everybody else's supposedly inexpensive moral obligations.

There is also a legitimate argument that educating and feeding children benefits society as a whole, including those of us who don't have children. Fair enough. Make that argument. Debate the economics, the benefits, the costs, the alternatives, and where the responsibility should ultimately lie.

**But screaming that I'm a bad person isn't an argument. It's an attempt to substitute moral intimidation for intellectual discussion.**

**And there's something particularly irritating about people congratulating themselves on their generosity while proposing to spend somebody else's money.**

You want to feed hungry children? Wonderful. Donate to a charity. Sponsor a school lunch program. Organize a fundraiser. Volunteer your time. Put your own money where your mouth is.

I might even voluntarily contribute.

But there is an enormous philosophical difference between choosing to help somebody and being compelled to help somebody under threat of government enforcement.

**Generosity is a virtue. Compulsion is not. And there is nothing particularly charitable about volunteering somebody else's wallet.**
